A computational study done through the Frontiers in Science summer research program at the Center for Theoretical Biological Physics, Rice University, in Dr. José N. Onuchic's group, mentored by Erdong Ding.

I used structure-based molecular dynamics simulations, run on Rice's NOTS computing cluster, to study how the 2′-deoxyguanosine riboswitch (PDB 3SKI) folds and how its environment shapes that process. Tracking the fraction of native contacts, *Q*, I compared when each region of the RNA forms relative to the molecule as a whole at 0, 2, and 5 mM MgCl₂, which showed the ionic environment shifting the pathway the riboswitch takes as it folds. Temperature had the strongest effect: as it increased, folding slowed sharply, and at many temperatures the riboswitch did not fold at all, even with the ligand and high magnesium present.
